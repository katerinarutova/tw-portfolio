# The Art of the SME Interview: How to Extract Knowledge

> "The biggest challenge in technical writing is not the writing itself, but extracting knowledge from people who have too little time and too much expertise."

A tech writer is a **translator between the world of code and the world of the user**.  
Clear documentation starts with one thing: getting the right information out of the right people.

This playbook is a practical method for turning a 20-minute SME call into something actually usable.

---

## Phase 1: Preparation (Don't waste their time)

Before asking anything, I do my homework. Respecting a developer’s time is non-negotiable.

* **Exploratory digging:** I go through the code—even if I don’t fully understand it. I need to know where things start and end.
* **Context hunting:** I read Jira tickets, Slack threads, and specs. I’m answering one question:  
  *“Why was this built, and what was the hardest part?”*
* **Drafting a skeleton:** I never show up with a blank page. I bring a deliberately imperfect draft. People fix things faster than they create them.

:::tip My rule
A bad draft beats an empty page every time.  
Experts switch into “fix mode” instantly—and that’s where the real information comes out.
:::

---

## Phase 2: The interview

Good interviews have rhythm. You ask, listen, adjust, and go deeper.

### Open-ended questions
Instead of:  
“Does this work via OAuth?”

Ask:  
> *“What process does a user go through when they authorize for the first time?”*

### The “Junior Developer” method
When the SME says:  
“It’s obvious, it just connects to our backend.”

I say:  
> *“Imagine I’m a developer on day one. What exactly do I need to type into the terminal to see a result?”*

### The power of silence
Wait 3 seconds longer than feels natural.  
Experts tend to fill the silence—and often reveal what they’d otherwise skip as “obvious.”

---

## Phase 3: Validation & async follow-up

The goal isn’t a long call. The goal is a clear outcome.

:::info Closing strategy
1. **Live confirmation:**  
   “So if I understand correctly, X does Y—but only if Z is met?”
2. **Async validation:**  
   Instead of long emails, I send a short Markdown summary via Slack or GitHub.
:::

> **Outcome for the client:**  
> Less time in meetings. More clarity. Documentation that’s ready to ship.
