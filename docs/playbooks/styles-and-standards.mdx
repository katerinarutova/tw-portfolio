# Styles and Standards: Why It Matters

When your team writes documentation, you need one thing above all: **consistency**.  
Not perfection. Not fancy words. Just **consistency**.

A style guide is the tool that keeps everything aligned.  
It tells your team:

- how to write  
- what terms to use  
- how to format content  
- how formal or informal the tone should be  

When everyone follows the same rules, the documentation becomes clearer, faster to read, and easier to maintain.

And yes — it saves a *ridiculous* amount of time.

---

## Why consistency matters

If multiple people write documentation (writers, developers, QA, support…), things fall apart fast.

One person writes **“Project”**,  
another writes **“Job”**,  
someone else writes **“Flow.”**

- Users get confused.  
- Support gets flooded with questions.  
- Documentation loses trust.

A style guide fixes this by providing **one source of truth**.

:::info
Consistency isn’t about rules for the sake of rules.  
It’s about making sure users don’t have to think twice.
:::

---

## How to create your style guide

You have three practical options:

### **1) Create your own guide from scratch**  
Best when your product has unique terminology or UI.

### **2) Use an existing standard**  
Examples:  
- Microsoft Writing Style Guide  
- AP Stylebook  
- Chicago Manual of Style  

### **3) Combine both**  
Define the parts that matter specifically to your product  
and let a standard guide cover everything else.

---

## You don’t need to define everything on day one

Most teams build their style guide gradually.  
Two or three pages are enough to start.

When something isn’t defined yet, simply state:

> **“If not defined here, follow the Microsoft Writing Style Guide.”**

That one sentence saves hours of arguments later.

---

## The important part

No matter which option you choose:

👉 **Everyone must follow the same rules.**  
That’s how you keep your documentation clean, predictable, and professional —  
no matter how many people contribute.

:::tip
A simple, incomplete style guide that everyone respects  
is infinitely better than a complex one nobody follows.
:::
